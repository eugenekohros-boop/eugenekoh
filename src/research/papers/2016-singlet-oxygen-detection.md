## The challenge

Studying singlet oxygen (¹O₂) in biology is difficult in many ways. It is an unstable, fleeting molecule, and it reacts readily with many biomolecules, which makes it hard to quantify accurately.

Most studies so far have focused on conditions that produce relatively *large* amounts of singlet oxygen, enough to cause obvious damage.

## What this review covers

We discuss the methods used to study singlet oxygen in biological systems and lay out their uses and limitations: what each approach can reliably tell you, and where it can mislead.

## Why it matters

Research is moving from the high singlet-oxygen levels that cause damage to the much lower levels involved in **signalling and regulation**, and that needs more sensitive methods. This review is a practical guide to choosing and interpreting detection methods. It is a companion to our [study of singlet oxygen in drought-induced cell death](/research/2016-vacuolar-cell-death/), published the same year, which combined three detection approaches: a fluorescent sensor, EPR spectroscopy and marker genes.
