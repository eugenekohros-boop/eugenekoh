## The question

Singlet oxygen is usually thought of as a product of light. It forms when photosynthesis cannot use all the energy it absorbs, or when chlorophyll precursors build up, as in the *flu* mutant. This light-driven singlet oxygen is thought to be involved in stress signalling and programmed cell death. But is singlet oxygen only a story about light and chloroplasts?

## What we did

We compared the transcriptomes of many stresses, applied in the light or in the dark, with the transcriptome of the *flu* mutant. From that comparison we defined a core gene set shared between singlet oxygen and other stresses, and tested it with the photosensitiser rose bengal. We then used the fluorescent probe Singlet Oxygen Sensor Green to look for singlet oxygen in the roots of stressed plants kept in the dark.

## What we found

- **A shared fingerprint.** Transcriptomes of many stresses, from both light and dark treatments, correlated with that of *flu*. We defined a **core set of 118 genes** common to singlet oxygen, biotic and abiotic stress, and confirmed that rose bengal switches them on through singlet oxygen.
- **No light needed.** Selected biotic and abiotic stresses switched on the core genes in the dark and in tissue that does not photosynthesise.
- **Seen in roots, in the dark.** The sensor detected rapid singlet-oxygen production in *Arabidopsis* roots under a range of stresses in darkness.
- **Many possible sources.** The sensor's signal built up in mitochondria, peroxisomes and the nucleus, suggesting several places where singlet oxygen may be made or may act.

## Why it matters

Singlet oxygen can be produced by several stress pathways, in compartments other than the chloroplast, without light. Its role in plant stress regulation is therefore much broader than previously thought. This set up the questions behind my later work on [how singlet oxygen damages cells](/research/2016-vacuolar-cell-death/), [how it signals through RNA](/research/2021-rna-oxidation-translation/), and [where in the cell it starts](/research/2022-singlet-oxygen-origins/).
