## The question

Since the start of the genomics era, high-throughput experiments have produced huge, varied datasets about how plants respond to stress. Data are plentiful, but there is so much of it, in so many forms, that analysing it has become a major challenge. Can recent advances in AI help turn this flood into knowledge?

## What the review covers

- **Data resources and tools.** An overview of the databases and bioinformatic tools available for studying plant stress.
- **What deep learning already does well.** Deep-learning models now predict gene structure, function and expression from DNA or protein sequence. They are also used widely in high-throughput phenomics in agriculture.
- **Where it is still early days.** Using these models to understand gene regulation and signalling, which lie at the heart of the stress response, is still in its infancy.
- **A hands-on demonstration.** We used a public large language model, ChatGPT, to build a **knowledge graph** of the experimental and computational methods used to study plant stress.

## Why it matters

Making sense of genomic, transcriptomic, proteomic, metabolomic and phenomic data needs computer scientists, computational biologists and plant scientists working together. This review is meant as a starting point for those collaborations. It maps the data, the tools and the open problems where AI could make the biggest difference.
